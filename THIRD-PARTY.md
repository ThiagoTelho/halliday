## OpenVR

https://github.com/ValveSoftware/openvr

The OpenVR interface layouts (method order of each interface version, from `openvr.h` at tags
v1.0.10, v1.0.12, v1.4.18 and v1.7.15), enum values and struct definitions, used by Halliday's OpenVR
runtime for Windows games under Wine (`core/halliday-vrclient`: `ovr.rs`, the interfaces in
`src/iface/`, and `src/iface/tables.rs`, generated from the headers by `tools/gen-openvr-tables.py`),
the SteamVR Input file formats (action manifest and bindings, read by `src/iface/input.rs`), and
the way `openvr_api.dll` finds a runtime (`openvrpaths.vrpath`, `bin/vrclient_x64.dll`,
`VRClientCoreFactory`, `IVRClientCore_003`).
The behavior behind the interfaces is Halliday's own; no runtime code is taken from SteamVR, Proton,
OpenComposite or xrizer.

## Klepton

https://github.com/shinyquagsire23/Klepton (Max Thomas), and the fork
https://github.com/armax9/Klepton-MoltenVR (with myMartek's AVP Play work)

What runs Quest apps (Android ARM64 packages) on Apple Silicon: a relinker and a compatibility
layer, with an OpenXR client for a runtime on the Mac. Not part of Halliday's sources: `make
klepton` builds the fork's runner from `reference/klepton-moltenvr` with
`tools/klepton-halliday.patch` (the runtime to load named by an environment variable; a target
and general Java answers for apps in two halves) and puts it in the app bundle (`Resources/quest/klepton-runner`),
beside MoltenVK (https://github.com/KhronosGroup/MoltenVK, Apache-2.0) and SDL3
(https://github.com/libsdl-org/SDL, zlib licence), both copied from Homebrew into the bundle so a
Mac needs neither installed. A build of Halliday without that step carries none of them. Klepton is MIT licensed (Copyright (c) 2026 Max
Thomas); its own third-party notices are in its `third-party-licenses/`.

## OpenZL

https://github.com/facebook/openzl (Meta Platforms, Inc. and affiliates)

A compression format some Quest apps pack their downloads with. Not part of Halliday's sources:
`make unzl` builds OpenZL from `reference/openzl` (with Zstandard and LZ4, which its Makefile
fetches) and links it into `tools/unzl.c`, a small unpacker the app bundle then carries
(`Resources/quest/unzl`). It unpacks OpenZL and Zstandard
(https://github.com/facebook/zstd) with them, and xz with Apple's Compression framework, part of
macOS. A build of Halliday without that step carries none of it. OpenZL is BSD licensed;
Zstandard is BSD licensed and LZ4's library BSD 2-Clause.

## Libraries compiled into Halliday

Halliday's Rust core links these into the app (Cargo fetches them when building):

- **libusb** (https://libusb.info, through `libusb1-sys`), the USB access to the headset: GNU
  Lesser General Public License 2.1 (its authors are in the bundle's `libusb-AUTHORS.txt`). It is
  built from its own unmodified source. Halliday's source is open and `make` builds it, so a
  modified libusb can be linked in its place: change the `libusb1-sys` dependency in
  `core/halliday-core/Cargo.toml` (or its vendored source) and build again.
- **rusb** (https://github.com/a1ien/rusb) and **libusb1-sys**: MIT.
- **libc** (https://github.com/rust-lang/libc) and **thiserror**
  (https://github.com/dtolnay/thiserror): MIT or Apache-2.0.
- **openxr-sys** (https://github.com/Ralith/openxrs), the OpenXR runtime's types: MIT or
  Apache-2.0.
